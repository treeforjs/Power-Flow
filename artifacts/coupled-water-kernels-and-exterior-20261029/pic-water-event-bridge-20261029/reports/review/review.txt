Two actionable numerical findings

1. [P2] A magnitude-only inverse-boost guard does not certify the incident direction
   (snapshot source/water_parent_bridge.hpp:33-45, especially 43).
   At 14 eV target-rest incidence along x, target proper velocity U=(0,1e5*c,0),
   sample() accepts. An independent 120-digit direct four-vector inverse boost,
   using exact binary64 inputs, gives direction-vector error 4.934636106675324e-8.
   Its returned unit-vector norm error is only 3.713280816981546e-18, so a norm
   check cannot detect this transverse error. The lab K+reference defect is
   -18.579443877060733 eV per reacting physical electron/target pair. The reported
   lab tolerance is 190.78274256250583 eV per pair because it scales with the
   enormous common translational kinetic energy. Thus its original lab bound is
   obeyed, while reaction-scale accuracy is unqualified. This is an extreme
   manufactured boost; it is not evidence of a failure for thermal water.

   Request: remove the ill-conditioned coefficient subtraction, bound component
   errors, and explicitly reject when the represented lab state cannot resolve
   the declared reaction-energy precision. Retain the original lab closure bound.
   A useful exact identity is u_rest=u_e-k*U, k=(gamma_e+gamma_rel)/(Gamma+1),
   gamma_rel=1+K_rest/(m_e*c^2). It follows from
   u_e dot U/c^2=gamma_e*Gamma-gamma_rel. Use a long-double FMA for each vector
   component. The error bound must include gamma, gamma_rel/energy rounding,
   division and coefficient errors, as well as final vector subtraction. This
   identity alone is not an admission/conditioning policy.

2. [P2] Weighted double conversion can bypass a failed conservation check
   (snapshot source/water_parent_bridge.hpp:105-111).
   At W_e=1e-300 and W_H2O=64e-300, a finite positive candidate rate exists.
   Both the weighted defect and tolerance narrow to binary64 zero. With axis
   (0,0,1+4e-13), which the kernel's <1e-12 axis-norm policy admits, the ordinary
   W_e=0.25 event rejects with 'parent lab energy closure'; the identical
   per-particle kinematics at tiny weight accepts with defect=bound=0.
   Acceptance therefore depends on packet scale after receipt underflow.
   Request: test unweighted long-double conservation before multiplying by the
   common event weight; reject unsupported weighted receipt conversion explicitly.
   Do not relax the original conservation threshold to pass this probe.

Physical model and mass/reference accounting

The target-rest-to-lab map to_lab() is the proper-velocity Lorentz boost. The
conditional kernel's CM model is internally consistent in exact arithmetic:
sqrt(s)=sqrt(((M+m_e)c^2)^2+2*M*c^2*K), available CM kinetic energy is
sqrt(s)-(M+m_e)c^2, and Q=available-I. The two electrons have opposite CM momenta
and equal CM kinetic energies Q/2; the ion is at rest in CM. Its rest mass is
M_ion=M-m_e+I/c^2, I=12.621 eV. The CM boost returns the total incoming momentum.
The lab bridge boosts all three products with the target's lab proper velocity.

Kinetic-only accounting requires sum(K_after)+I=K_before per reacting pair.
The chemical reference is already the added rest-mass energy, not an additional
heat deposit. A full total-energy mc^2*gamma ledger must not add I a second time.
Product.reference_J is per physical particle; bridge defect/limit receipts are
already multiplied by event_weight. The canonical native owner must retain the
declared product mass and apply the same reference convention. Binary64 rest-mass
rounding leaves a small difference between the exact stored mass difference and I
(this fixture: -1.6933563728743732e-8 eV); this is a quantified representation
effect, not evidence of double counting. The sealed native electron constant
matches bridge me=9.1093837139e-31 kg.

Support and missing physics remain explicit. The pinned parent partial-ion curve
is 13.5..1000 eV with zero below12.621 eV and a rejected12.621..13.5 eV gap.
The only selected model is parent_ground_equal_pair_CM_v1. One intact surviving
singly charged parent is interpreted as one such event; the differential closure
is an explicit equal-pair/isotropic comparison, not a measured secondary spectrum.
No dissociative/double/core ionization, neutral fragmentation, vibrational/internal
branching, or above1keV product closure is supplied. A retained~8keV tail remains
unsupported. No EEDF, breakdown, foil halo, or full-water acceptance follows.

Stale-state and native integration gaps (caller ownership, not extra bridge claims)

sample() binds IDs, states, co-cell membership, original weights, selected data and
kernel hashes, compensated remainders, and recomputed E/flux/sigma/rate. It correctly
rejects an inconsistent changed volume without the corresponding rate. However it
reconstructs the authoritative cell from event.volume_m3 itself: coherently changing
volume and rate passes. Rotating a stationary-target projectile at unchanged speed
passes, as does changing only target mass. These examples demonstrate that pair-rate
consistency is not authoritative Clock/state/mesh freshness. The bridge owns no
epoch/geometry and cannot discover these changes by itself. The host must bind
and validate the actual frozen stage, complete packet state/revision, cell volume,
mesh, source bytes and selected proposal before common publication. Preserve the
separate physical stage and collision-subflow elapsed time; no orbit was advanced
to the sampled SSA waiting time.

Unequal-weight physical first moment remains min(W_e,W_n)*max(W_e,W_n)*sigma*g/V.
Both electrons and the ion use the reacting min-weight; uncompensated reactant
remainders are returned separately. The low limb survives the bridge. Packet input
still has one-double weight: iterative compensated neutral/native storage, its
propensity input, count/charge/chemical ledgers and restart are not implemented.
The native owner must preserve low limbs end to end or fail the whole event/step
before physical/RNG publication. Silently dropping a low limb or resampling around
the failed event is not acceptable. Product IDs/positions/cell placement, compatible
charge/current deposition, species ownership, boundary collection, field work,
native rollback and atomic publication remain integration work.

Separate scheduler work-budget recommendation

The pinned candidates() loops every projectile x target before skipping unequal
cells. pair_limit counts only same-cell pairs. Sparse disjoint populations can
perform O(N_e*N_n) uncounted comparisons even with pair_limit=0 and no candidate.
Matching-state channel queries and output allocations are independently bounded,
but unmatched-channel traversal and the linear cell-volume lookup also cost work.
Use a cell-indexed join with authoritative cell-volume lookup and per-state channel
groups, preserving canonical output sort and unchanged relative/rate/remainder
calculations. Count index/input work, visited local pairs, actual queries, channel
traversal if retained, and allocated rows explicitly. Do not benchmark this by
merely checking an empty result or a small wall time.

Suggested regression contract: compare all Candidate fields bitwise against the
original builder for bounded seeded multi-cell/state/channel inputs and input
permutations, with threshold/coverage errors and zero-flux queries unchanged.
Test exact/one-below pair, query and row budgets independently. Instrument visits:
for e.g.1000 projectile and1000 neutral packets in disjoint cells, zero local-pair
visits and zero queries must occur after bounded linear/index work, rather than
one million hidden pair comparisons. Include dense one-cell inputs where the local
pair budget still rejects before excess kinematics/query work. This is a scheduling
performance correction only; no cross section, rate or physical scope change.
