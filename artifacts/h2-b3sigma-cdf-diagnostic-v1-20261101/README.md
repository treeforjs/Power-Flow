# H2 b3Sigma dissociation with a primary fragment-energy distribution

This isolated CPU diagnostic samples e+H2(X1Sigma_g,v=0) → e+H(1s)+H(1s) using the published fragment-energy inverse CDF and the unchanged primary MCCC integral. It creates actual energy- and momentum-conserving events through the corrected shared chemistry kernel. It does not enable a native channel or qualify the full H2 encounter group.

The fragment-energy source is E. Sartori, “Energy distribution of fragments from H2 and H2+ dissociation by electron impact for the use in numerical models applied to negative ion sources,” JINST 19 C03026 (2024), DOI [10.1088/1748-0221/19/03/C03026](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-0221/19/03/C03026). [Publisher Table I](https://content.cld.iop.org/journals/1748-0221/19/03/C03026/revision1/table_I.txt), SHA 3013c05f2c0913cfa6a784d817bb7b87733af67b395e8cc273da2eaa48abff91, supplies 100 inverse-CDF knots for the b3Sigma_u channel. Only its v=0 column is used. The paper and supplement are CC BY 4.0; this implementation attributes the author, title, journal and DOI, and records its changes below. The paper is a primary Franck–Condon/delta approximation, not measured fragment spectra or a fully differential scattering calculation.

The supplied quantiles cover 0.001–0.999, despite the paper's description of zero-to-one sampling. Per-H fragment energies span 1.342054–5.32797 eV. The omitted lower and upper probability tails total 0.002. They are never extrapolated, clamped or called zero. The diagnostic explicitly conditions on the published central interval and further conditions on exact finite-target collision-CM energy. It samples uniformly within the retained quantile interval, then linearly interpolates source fragment energy. These conditional distributions are renormalized; the physical uncertainty of omitting the tails is not qualified.

Each source energy is the kinetic energy of one H in the fragment-pair rest frame. The sampled pair KER is twice that value. The dynamic electron energy loss is the unchanged registry chemical cost, 4.478069261841885 eV, plus this pair KER. The existing solver handles finite-target electron recoil, exact two-body fragment breakup and moving-target Lorentz boosts. Scattered-electron directions are assumed isotropic in collision CM; fragment breakup is assumed isotropic in pair rest frame. Ground H products carry no hidden internal-energy sink. These angular assumptions are explicit diagnostic models.

The event-support threshold is 7.164126535758105 eV. Below it, the source's lowest retained fragment energy cannot fit the collision energy and the event refuses. The MCCC integral remains positive and unchanged over its actual retained 4.5–150 eV knots. Consequently the 4.5–7.164126535758105 eV portion remains event-unsupported; no invented threshold segment, shifted rate, or silent known-zero process is introduced. Energies outside 4.5–150 eV refuse even for integral evaluation. The integral is the primary calculated b3Sigma dissociative-excitation result, DOI [10.1016/j.adt.2020.101361](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.adt.2020.101361), source SHA 9bf47765e4e381e272b1ff4fa03108c06b95b655584912a426893cb5f0f0a732. Conditional KER normalization does not rescale that integral.

Random sampling uses the qualified disjoint layout 2, partition word 96, distinct from target words 4–19 and ordinary kinematic words 32–95. No old fixed-seed trajectory parity is claimed. Explicit diagnostic mode, target-rest energy frame, fixture-purpose key, finite target velocity and unit incoming direction are required.

Independent 70-digit controls verified finite-target recoil, physical product momenta, each H's pair-rest energy, source-CDF interpolation/conditional normalization and source-rate interpolation.
