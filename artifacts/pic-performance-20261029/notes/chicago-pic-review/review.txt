CHICAGO reference studies for a heated pulsed-power A-K gap
Primary-source review checked 2026-10-29

CHICAGO is a well-supported reference code for this problem family. The closest
published comparisons require matching a particular geometry, drive circuit,
surface inventory, emission prescription and chemistry closure. A code name by
itself does not specify those choices. Nichelle L. Bennett is listed as N. Bennett
on the principal journal papers below. No simulations or production changes were
performed for this review. No local thesis/RGA data were interpreted.

The present model advances 3D electron trajectories using prescribed magnetic
fields and a height-averaged 2D Poisson solve, an imposed conditional voltage,
Richardson thermal supply, and no gas, ions, collisions, melt or plasma feedback.
The following CHICAGO studies evolve electromagnetic fields and particle/plasma
currents together. Their emission models generally include field-triggered SCL
emission and contaminant plasma. Their plasma results therefore cannot be used
as numerical validation of the present collisionless electron-only approximation
without first defining a common restricted case.

Eight prioritized works

[1] Derek C. Lamppa, Sean C. Simpson, Brian T. Hutsel, George R. Laity,
Michael E. Cuneo, David V. Rose (October 2021).
Assessment of Electrode Contamination Mitigation at 0.5 MA Scale.
SAND2021-12691. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2172/1825219
Primary full text:
https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/293/2025/02/Lamppa-2021-SAND-Report-SAND2021-12691.pdf
Closest finite-electrode geometry: Appendix C, printed pp.53-56, uses 3D
Cartesian CHICAGO for Mykonos parallel plates, half-volume symmetry, actual
current-path hardware, Ohmic/impact heating, field-triggered cathode SCL emission,
temperature-triggered anode ions and Temkin water-plasma sourcing. Open perimeter
transport changes accumulation relative to coaxial lines. Nominal 0.5/1.0 mm
calculations are compared with ICCD plasma structure/turn-on trends. The report
explicitly distinguishes integrated ion density from self-emission intensity;
this is qualitative morphology/trend support, not calibrated electron-density or
EEDF validation. Appendix D separately uses an idealized 2D cylindrical geometry.
This is the first geometry benchmark to reproduce before treating a strip/foil
cross-section as equivalent to an axisymmetric line.

[2] N. Bennett, D. R. Welch, K. Cochrane, K. Leung, C. Thoma,
M. E. Cuneo, G. Frye-Mason (25 April 2023).
Electrode plasma formation and melt in Z-pinch accelerators.
Physical Review Accelerators and Beams 26, 040401.
DOI/full text: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.26.040401
https://journals.aps.org/prab/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.26.040401
Most relevant chemistry/feedback study: 2D cylindrical relativistic EM Monte
Carlo PIC follows H2/H/ions, surface heating, neutral desorption, impact ionization,
momentum/charge exchange and Coulomb collisions. It uses 4 um cells for kinetic
breakdown; an approximate immediate-plasma comparison uses 10 um cells. It
demonstrates screening, changing particle energies and delayed ionization versus
desorption. This is detailed hydrogen kinetics, not validated full H2O chemistry.
Upstream plasma influx and terminal accumulation are omitted in this breakdown
case; bulk melt is studied with complementary MHD. Its curves are model/refinement
benchmarks, not an experimental EEDF measurement. See Secs.III A-C and Figs.5-9.

[3] N. Bennett, D. R. Welch, G. Laity, D. V. Rose, M. E. Cuneo
(23 June 2021).
Magnetized particle transport in multi-MA accelerators.
Physical Review Accelerators and Beams 24, 060401.
DOI/full text: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.24.060401
https://journals.aps.org/prab/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.24.060401
Fully relativistic EM kinetic simulations connect plasma density/energy and
Hall-related cross-gap transport in Z geometries. Electrons, ions and neutrals
advance kinetically with collisions and arbitrary distributions. Crucially,
surface molecules are immediately fragmented and ionized: molecular breakup
delay is not resolved. H and O represent light/heavy constituents; selected
excitation and higher charge states are omitted. Field-triggered cathode SCL
emission, thermal anode ions and Joule/particle heating are included. Comparisons
include simulated cross-gap current against Hall-current estimates and geometry/
load-inductance trends. Agreement of an integral current does not validate a
low-energy EEDF or species branching. See Sec.III A-B and Sec.V.

[4] D. R. Welch, N. Bennett, T. C. Genoni, D. V. Rose, C. Thoma,
C. Miller, W. A. Stygar (8 July 2019).
Electrode contaminant plasma effects in 10^7-A Z pinch accelerators.
Physical Review Accelerators and Beams 22, 070401.
DOI/full text: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.22.070401
https://journals.aps.org/prab/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.22.070401
2D fully kinetic EM inner-MITL models include Ohmic/particle heating and Temkin
water supply. Desorbed water is assumed to produce 3 electrons, 2 H+ and O+
within a grid cell; rapid dissociation/ionization is a closure, not a resolved
reaction sequence. It compares current delivery/loss for geometries, adsorbate
coverage and applied magnetic insulation, and checks the magnetic-implicit orbit
algorithm. Bulk-metal vaporization is excluded; the water-dominance and rapid
ionization assumptions are explicitly identified for further work. Useful
benchmark observables are loss-current waveforms, surface/volume plasma density
and the same coverage sweep, with the closure declared. See Sec.II A and Sec.III.

[5] D. R. Welch, N. Bennett, T. C. Genoni, C. Thoma, D. V. Rose
(3 November 2020).
Fast hybrid particle-in-cell technique for pulsed-power accelerators.
Physical Review Accelerators and Beams 23, 110401.
DOI/full text: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.23.110401
https://journals.aps.org/prab/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.23.110401
EM kinetic, multifluid and particle-migration hybrid calculations compare orbit,
plasma-density and current-loss results. Dense thermal material can be fluid while
energetic particles remain kinetic; examples use a 50 keV transition. Integral
current agreement can coexist with loss of sheath vortices. Underresolved plasma
frequencies omit high-frequency interactions. This provides numerical reference
cases, not permission to enlarge the current explicit Boris timestep or replace
the low-energy EEDF by a fluid temperature. Reproduce the Larmor/E x B tests and
compare distribution functions before considering analogous acceleration.

[6] Nichelle L. Bennett, Dale Welch (October 2022; Sandia catalog dates it
1 October 2022).
Resolution requirements for energy conservation in kinetic plasma simulations.
SAND2022-13998. DOI: https://doi.org/10.2172/1891696
Primary accessible summary:
https://www.sandia.gov/research/publications/details/resolution-requirements-for-energy-conservation-in-kinetic-plasma-simulatio-2022-10-01/
CHICAGO energy-conserving implicit PIC tests an unforced drifting plasma in 2D,
using 400 particles/cell and 50,000 timesteps. Reported skin-depth-based grid
limits differ by particle shape order and chosen energy-error criterion. This
is a controlled numerical conservation benchmark with specified solver, shapes,
population and timestep, not a universal allowable mesh size for another PIC
formulation. It does not establish convergence of surface sheaths, EEDF tails or
reaction rates. The Sandia summary was inspected; OSTI full text was unavailable
through the web reader during this review.

[7] Nathan Hines, Derek Lamppa, Jens Schwarz, Michael Cuneo,
Thomas Awe, Mark Gilmore (7-11 October 2024).
MITL electrode surface plasma density measurements at Sandia's 1 MA accelerator
Mykonos. SAND2024-13691C, APS Division of Plasma Physics presentation.
Primary full text:
https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/293/2025/02/2024-10-APS-DPP-Presentation-SAND2024-13691C.pdf
Experimental diagnostic benchmark, not a CHICAGO validation paper. SHOP-DI
measures free-electron areal density versus time; reported sensitivity is
6.3e13 cm^-2. Preliminary geometry scans compare plasma detection with calculated
thermal and field-enhanced onset times. Field-enhanced/explosive emission is
considered as an explanation; it is not an independently proven universal trigger.
Compare synthetic line-integrated density along the same chord and detection
threshold, not a local cm^-3 pixel. The finite probe position and expansion delay
separate detection time from creation time. See slides6-10.

[8] T. M. Watson, D. C. Lamppa, N. Bennett, D. Welch, T. Smith,
J. T. Banasek, N. R. Hines, J. Schwarz, J. P. Van Devender (June 2025).
Investigation of Magnetically Insulated Transmission Lines Surface Plasma
Temperature with a Novel Miniature X-ray Detector.
SAND2025-01647A, 25th IEEE Pulsed Power and Plasma Science Conference abstract.
Primary abstract:
https://www.sandia.gov/app/uploads/sites/293/2025/03/Watson-PPPS-2025-Abstract.pdf
miniXRD observations target spatially varying surface-plasma temperature and
turn-on, including Mykonos and Z PF17/PF18. The abstract explicitly proposes the
turn-on data as a CHICAGO benchmark. Its temperature inference assumes continuum
radiation and the Mykonos results suggest an axial nonequilibrium distribution.
This is an announced benchmark opportunity, not a published quantitative
simulation-data agreement or a measurement of the complete EEDF. Obtain detector
response, line/continuum separation, view geometry and uncertainty before using
its inferred temperature to constrain electron distributions.

Supporting sources for a foil/water benchmark

[S1] T. J. Smith et al., Understanding electrode plasma formation on wires and
thin foils via vacuum ultraviolet spectroscopy of desorbed surface contaminants,
SAND2024-02100C (2024), DOI https://doi.org/10.2172/2540452
https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2540452
The primary poster covers planar foil loads, a heating/desorption PDE model,
VUV hydrogen diagnostics and visible spectra. It is close to thin-foil hardware,
but the poster does not document a CHICAGO run. Do not present it as CHICAGO
validation. The related 2023 abstract has different authors/report metadata;
do not merge the versions.

DOI https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0035315
Author repository, version of record:

Primary evaluated cross-section reference covering scattering, momentum transfer,

[S4] N. Bennett, D. R. Welch, C. A. Jennings, E. Yu, M. H. Hess,
B. T. Hutsel, G. Laity, J. K. Moore, D. V. Rose, K. Peterson,
M. E. Cuneo (10 December 2019).
Current transport and loss mechanisms in the Z accelerator.
Physical Review Accelerators and Beams 22, 120401.
DOI https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.22.120401
https://journals.aps.org/prab/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevAccelBeams.22.120401
This adds quantitative experiment/theory comparisons: upstream currents from two
Z shots agree with coupled simulations within 2%; Fig.13 compares load currents
with measurement uncertainty of +/-5%. A 40 kA coaxial MITL resolution test
compares integrated E against a laminar-flow theory relation; mesh refinement
fixes artificial sheath broadening while timestep refinement alone does not.
The EM circuit-coupled model includes adaptive particle management that limits
species/cell counts only when charge, energy and momentum distributions can be
preserved. This supports controlled particle management, not arbitrary thinning
or guaranteed EEDF preservation. See Sec.II B and Figs.5,6,13.

Recommended benchmark sequence (proposed work, not completed validation)

A. Match the restricted collisionless problem first. Create a common finite
electrode case with identical voltage history, magnetic-field history, emission
law, imposed/source charge, boundaries and initial particles. Compare particle
orbits, E x B drift, electrode flux, energy spectra, charge conservation and
height/line sampling. This determines numerical disagreement before adding
physics. A published full-EM CHICAGO current-loss curve is not the corresponding
reference for a prescribed-B Poisson approximation. Separate errors caused by
field evolution, electrostatic geometry and particle sampling.

B. Reproduce the finite parallel-plate reference [1]. Use its actual dimensions,
driver circuit, symmetry, emission-enabled surfaces and open perimeters. Use a
documented water-plasma closure initially, then finite-rate chemistry. Compare
turn-on, plasma extent and current transfer at common drive times. Forward-model
ICCD emission before comparing light intensity; use line integrals for SHOP-DI
[7]. Neither a 2D cylindrical movie nor a height-averaged density field by itself
tests finite-electrode escape and axial nonuniformity.

C. Reproduce a finite-rate breakdown case [2] as specified in the paper, using
the hydrogen-only species set and boundary impedance. Compare neutral depletion,
ionization fraction, local particle-energy distributions, screened E profiles and
ion/electron wall energy flux. Then compare its immediate-ionization closure on
the same drive. This isolates the chemistry-delay assumption without pretending
that H2 kinetics validates water fragmentation. The 4 um published resolution is
a reference setup to refine, not evidence that 25 um is sufficient here.

D. Add a water reaction benchmark with fixed sources and fields before feedback.
Use a controlled monoenergetic electron beam through uniform H2O to check each
channel probability 1-exp(-n sigma v dt), branching, electron multiplication,
reaction energy loss, atom/charge balance and weighted-particle bookkeeping.
Next use prescribed spatially varying H2O from both finite electrodes. Track
H2O, OH, O, H and relevant molecular/atomic ions and negative-ion channels when
supported by the selected dataset and energy regime. Declare every omitted
channel. Do not turn one molecule directly into 2H+ + O+ + 3e unless explicitly
testing that fast-plasma approximation. Secondary electrons must enter the EEDF.

For stationary neutrals, use the local rate for reaction r,

    R_r(x,t) = n_H2O(x,t) n_e(x,t) integral[sigma_r(E) v(E) f_E(E,x,t) dE],

where f_E is normalized to one; with moving neutrals use relative collision
energy/velocity. A gap-averaged EEDF or mean energy substituted into sigma cannot
capture the overlap of hot electrons and dense near-surface water. Save EEDF and
pitch-angle distributions by physical region/height/time, high-energy tail
fractions, channel-resolved rates, fragment densities and ion energy/angular
fluxes. Report local densities in cm^-3, chord integrals in cm^-2 and spectra
with normalization and weighted effective sample size. Compare actual rate
integrals, not only fitted temperatures.

E. Enable feedback in declared stages. First ion space charge and ion transport;
then ion/electron surface heating, desorption depletion/resupply, secondary or
stimulated emission where justified, magnetic/current feedback and electrode
motion/melt/erosion. Check changes in sheath potential, electron residence time,
EEDF cooling/tail replenishment, ionization geography, wall energy deposition and
load-current waveform at every stage. A hot electron-only cloud cannot predict
plasma current shunting or ion-driven source feedback. A thermal field past melt
without phase change needs an explicit validity boundary.

Required experimental inputs and comparison limits

Use measured circuit/current/voltage timing with their locations and load
inductance, not only nominal peak voltage. Specify foil alloy/thickness, oxide,
roughness, cleaning, pumpdown/heating history, initial surface coverage and
background gas separately. An RGA water signal needs cracking-pattern, species
sensitivity, pumping/conductance and timing calibration before it becomes a
surface inventory or spatial gas source; detector fragments are not automatically
the plasma fragment fractions. Preserve measured anode/cathode differences
instead of imposing mirrored temperatures when testing actual hardware.

Numerical convergence must cover particle count/weight, mesh, timestep, source
patch/event sampling, collision probability substepping and chemistry inventory.
Use uncertainty on both simulated observables and experimental inversion. Hybrid
current agreement [5] and global energy stability [6] do not guarantee low-energy
EEDF or reaction-rate accuracy. No reviewed source supplies a matched input deck
and calibrated EEDF/H2O-fragmentation dataset for the present finite foil geometry;
those remain required benchmark inputs rather than inferred evidence.
