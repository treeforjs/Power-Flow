ADDITIONAL SURFACE/MATERIAL REQUEST — retained with the audit

Requested direction: allow solid electrode material to become released vapor at
melting after sufficient deposited energy, and account for oxide and other
surface layers beneath the adsorbed water layers.

Working interpretation: an explicitly reduced melt-triggered, energy-limited
ablation closure. Reach the declared melting/phase state, then pay a specified
removal energy for each actual unit of released mass. Melting alone must not
instantaneously replace an entire cell with vapor. Fusion energy already in
caloric enthalpy must not be charged twice. Removal energy, gas reference,
actual kinetic/internal energy, solid enthalpy and any retained liquid must
share a single extensive energy convention. For a simple positive incremental
release cost L_eff, delta_m <= min(m_available, Q_available/L_eff); the actual
transaction must use complete reference-state differences, not that shorthand
if product temperature/species or the retained state change.

Keep finite surface inventories for exposed/retained metal, oxide, identified
other contaminants, and adsorbed water, with explicit area and coverage units.
Do not invent thicknesses, stoichiometries, thermal resistances, layer order or
release enthalpies absent from the recovered evidence. A thermally thin surface
inventory representation can be considered; resolving every nanometre layer in
the bulk mesh is not automatically required. Unknowns can be declared scenario
parameters with sensitivity bounds, not described as measured properties.

Water/contaminant desorption need not wait for melting. Each species retains its
own desorption/reaction law and finite reservoir; the metal-release criterion
is not a universal release temperature for oxide, hydrocarbons or hydroxyls.
Releasing a neutral metal/oxide species does not immediately ionize it. Metal
kinetics cannot borrow H2O cross sections. Surface exposure can change electron
barrier/work function, dielectric charging and thermal/field boundary response;
any fixed-property simplification must be declared and bounded.

Geometry/recession, mass, heat, charge/current and particle birth/collection must
be one common transaction. A temperature clamp without paying removal energy,
or a fixed-conducting region that silently retains removed mass, would not be
this model. This new closure needs its own version and tests; it does not extend
the existing 3000 K or mechanical validity seals by relabeling them.
